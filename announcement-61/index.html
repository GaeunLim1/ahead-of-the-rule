China Announcement 61

MOFCOM requires a licence for foreign-made items with 0.1% or more Chinese rare-earth content. Its list names samarium-cobalt magnets, neodymium magnets containing terbium or dysprosium, the parts and modules that contain them, and rare-earth sputtering targets.

As of 15 October 2026

{% include stage-strip.html case="ann61" %}

Dependency: China held 91% of magnet rare-earth refining (Nd, Pr, Dy, Tb) and 94% of sintered magnet production in 2024 (IEA).

Firm impact

The content share travels down the chain with the goods. The liability stays with each exporter.

ItemContent
High exposureMakers of HDDs, fans and motors using terbium or dysprosium magnets; chipmakers using rare-earth sputtering targets
InformationThe content share is passed down on the Declaration of Compliance, from each supplier to the next recipient
CostA licence for each export of a covered item; switching to magnets without terbium or dysprosium, or from outside China
AllocationThe exporter applies for the licence. The Declaration does not exempt anyone from due diligence
No contract fixesA delayed licence with no alternative supply, because dependency is high
