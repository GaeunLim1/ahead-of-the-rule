China Announcement 61

MOFCOM requires a licence for foreign-made items with 0.1% or more Chinese rare-earth content. Its list names samarium-cobalt magnets, neodymium magnets containing terbium or dysprosium, the parts and modules that contain them, and rare-earth sputtering targets. In a fab, that means the magnets inside vacuum pumps, wafer robots and linear stages, and the targets used in deposition.

Dependency: China held 91% of magnet rare-earth refining (Nd, Pr, Dy, Tb) and 94% of sintered magnet production in 2024 (IEA).

Scope, now and after the pause

The April 2025 controls are not paused. What the pause holds back is the reach beyond China's border.

ItemIn force nowWhen the pause ends
ElementsSm, Gd, Tb, Dy, Lu, Sc, Y (April 2025)Adds Ho, Er, Tm, Eu, Yb (Ann. 57)
Where it appliesExports from ChinaAlso exports of foreign-made items from any country, Hong Kong included (Ann. 61)
What is caughtRare-earth metals, oxides, alloys and some magnetsAlso parts and modules with listed magnets, and rare-earth targets, at 0.1% of value
Equipment and technologyNot in the April 2025 controlsRare-earth equipment and materials (Ann. 56); rare-earth technology (Ann. 62)
Users and end usesStandard end-user reviewListed users and their 50%-controlled subsidiaries refused in principle; tools and materials for logic at 14 nm or below, or memory at 256 layers or more, reviewed case by case

Parts 1(a) and 1(b) of Ann. 61 were due to take effect on 1 December 2025. Whether they apply at once when the pause ends, or after a new date, will depend on the notice that ends it.

Firm impact

The content share travels down the chain with the goods. The liability stays with each exporter.

ItemContent
High exposureMakers and distributors of vacuum pumps, wafer robots, linear motors and stages using terbium, dysprosium or samarium-cobalt magnets; suppliers of rare-earth sputtering targets
InformationThe content share is passed down on the Declaration of Compliance; the end use, process node and layer count, sits with the fab
CostA licence for each export of a covered item; case-by-case review for leading-edge tools and materials; process information disclosed to MOFCOM
AllocationThe exporter applies, or has an intermediary agency, chamber of commerce or industry association apply for it. The Declaration does not exempt anyone from due diligence
No contract fixesA delayed licence for a spare part stops a tool, and dependency on China for the magnets is high

Information trace

The content share comes down with the goods. The end use has to go up to the licence application. The distributor sits between the two and holds neither.

{% include trace-table.html rule="ann61" %}

Each exporter must hand the next recipient a Declaration of Compliance stating the share of value that controlled Chinese rare earth makes up. The recipient may ask for it. A part with more than 0.1% that "can be used independently" is caught in its own right, but the notice does not say whether that is the pump or the tool the pump goes into.

The flow also runs to MOFCOM. A licence application names the end user and the end use, and for leading-edge chipmaking that means the process node or layer count. Ann. 61 also turns intermediaries into a channel: agencies, chambers of commerce and industry associations may apply on an exporter's behalf.
