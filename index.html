Two export controls and the chipmaking equipment supply chain

Export control analysis by Gaeun Lim. As of 19 October 2026.

Information moves. Liability stays.

Both rules make a firm answer for something it cannot see by itself: who owns its customer, or how much Chinese rare earth is inside what it sells. That information sits with another firm in the chain. This site traces where it sits, who is liable for it, and where the two come apart. Where they come apart is the gap, and the cost of closing it is pushed onto whoever has the least leverage.

Where the two rules meet

A distributor buys a vacuum pump with Chinese terbium magnets and resells it to a fab that two listed parties together own 50% or more of. One shipment needs a MOFCOM licence and a BIS licence. For the first, the content share comes down from the pump maker and the end use has to go up from the fab. For the second, the fab's ownership has to go up to the distributor. The distributor holds none of it.

The unit is a node in the chain. At each node the same five questions apply: is it caught, what information does it need, who holds that information, who is liable, and what is left that no contract can fix. A node that is liable for information it does not hold is marked as a gap and given a type.

This maps where the gaps are, not how large they are. Measures such as the share of intermediaries on the Entity List, the number of tiers in a bill of materials, or the share of magnets sourced from China can be added later. Risks that are not about information, such as having no other supplier, stay in the last row of each firm impact table.
