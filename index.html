Stage 1 · Target of the Rule

What the text names as its target. Read from the article itself, before anything downstream is considered.

TypeMeasured onExample
Attribute-based A continuous value measured on the item itself: content share, technical lineage, an aggregated count. A de minimis threshold on controlled US-origin content, measured by value; the calculated parameters written into a control list entry (e.g. 9E503.l.).
Relational A position in a network: nationality, headquarters location, a contract chain, a control relationship. Headquarters in a restricted jurisdiction; a re-export clause running through a third-country buyer; ownership of 50% or more by a listed entity, extending a listing to unlisted affiliates.
Conduct-based A future behavior the text conditions on, not a present property. A disciplinary commitment made as a condition of a suspended denial order; a duty to resolve a red flag or obtain a license once a party has knowledge of a listed owner.

Stage 2 · Range of the Rule

Not whether the rule existed before the case. Whether this rulemaking sits inside the agency's authorizing text, or redraws that line, and separately, what fires it.

Axis 1: scope of authority

TypeReading
Within the existing lineThe rulemaking moves inside the range already handed to the agency. Lower legal exposure once in force.
Redrawing the lineThe rulemaking extends the agency's own reach: a new jurisdictional or definitional line, not a new number inside an old one.

Read the reach against the authorizing text and stated aim, cited directly rather than summarized. If the operative text's actual reach matches those two, it stays within the existing line. If it pulls in a new class of actor that neither covers, it redraws the line.

Axis 2: trigger mechanism

TypeReading
Numeric triggerFires on a threshold or a calendar date, independent of any actor's behavior.
Conduct triggerFires on a breach or a failure to perform a condition. Creates a latent interval between the breach and its detection that a numeric trigger cannot produce.

Stage 3 · Multi-Jurisdictional Regulation

Read rules from different jurisdictions that apply to the same item, party, or transaction: what each one asks and how the answers relate.

What each rule asks

Target typeScope direction
Jurisdiction AIts Stage 1 type, carried forwardIts Stage 2 direction, carried forward
Jurisdiction BIts Stage 1 type, carried forwardIts Stage 2 direction, carried forward

Matching target types or matching scope directions mean the two readings tend to land in the same place. Differing answers mean they diverge from the start, usually without either jurisdiction having coordinated with the other.

How the answers relate

RelationReading
Separate reachThe two jurisdictions attach to different facts (where the item is, where it was made, who the party is, who owns it, where the technology is released), so each rule is answered on its own. Satisfying one says nothing about the other.
Same fact, two listsBoth jurisdictions ask about the same item or party, each against its own list, so the question is put twice and the answers can differ.
HandoffWhat happens under one jurisdiction, such as an export, creates the condition on which the other jurisdiction attaches.

Identify where a rule's structure can affect a firm's supply chain.

VariableReading
Position in the Supply ChainWhere does the affected firm, item, counterparty, or transaction sit in the supply chain?
Supply Chain DependencyHow dependent is the firm on the affected connection, supplier, customer, item, or route?
Available SubstitutesCan the affected supplier, customer, item, route, or process be replaced?
Time to Identify the IssueHow long does it take to determine whether the rule affects the relevant supply-chain connection?
Time to RespondHow much time is available to respond once the rule is found to affect the relevant supply-chain connection?
Advance PreparationCan the firm identify and prepare for the potential disruption in advance?
Resolution PointWho or what can resolve the issue and restore the supply-chain connection?
