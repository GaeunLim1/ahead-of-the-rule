Stage 1 · Target of the Rule

What the rule targets and the criteria through which that target is identified. Read the target before considering how the rule interacts with other rules or affects a firm's position.

TypeMeasured onExample
Attribute-based An attribute or measurable property of the item, data, technology, or activity itself, such as content share, technical lineage, or an aggregated count. A minimum content threshold by weight or value; a bulk-data threshold measured in persons (e.g. 100+ individuals' genomic records).
Relational A relationship to another party, jurisdiction, or point in a transaction chain, such as nationality, headquarters location, contractual connection, ownership, or control. Headquarters in a restricted jurisdiction; a re-export clause running through a third-country buyer; corporate affiliation extending to subsidiaries, parents, and successor entities.
Conduct-based An act, omission, undertaking, or required behavior on which the rule's operation or a condition depends, rather than solely on a present property. A disciplinary commitment made as a condition of a suspended denial order; a domestic-handling certification submitted as a condition of marketing approval.

Stage 2 · Design of the Rule

How the rule operates through its authority, how far the rule reaches, and triggering conditions. Examine the relationship between the scope of authority and the scope of application separately from the conditions that activate, change, or terminate the rule.

Axis 1: authority and how far the rule reaches

RelationshipReading
Within the Scope The how far the rule reaches appears to remain within the identified authorizing scope.
Scope Extension The how far the rule reaches appears to extend to additional actors, items, transactions, jurisdictions, or circumstances beyond the identified scope.
Scope Relationship Unclear The relationship between the identified authority and the how far the rule reaches cannot yet be determined from the dimensions examined.

Read the how far the rule reaches against the authorizing text and stated aim. Examine the authorizing basis, delegated scope, actors and circumstances covered, and the boundaries of application. Distinguish a change in the reach of the rule from a change in a threshold or condition within an existing scope.

Axis 2: what triggers the rule

TypeReading
Attribute or threshold trigger Triggered when an item, data set, transaction, or other measurable characteristic reaches a specified threshold.
Temporal trigger Triggered or altered by an effective date, deadline, specified period, or other time-dependent condition.
Status or listing trigger Triggered by a designation, list entry, classification, authorization status, or change in status.
Conduct or performance trigger Triggered by an act, omission, breach, or failure to satisfy a condition. The interval between the triggering conduct and its detection may create a determination gap.
Relationship or transaction trigger Triggered when a specified relationship, transaction, transfer, or other connection arises.
Based on a Determination Triggered or altered by an agency determination, classification, designation, or other specified decision.

A rule may involve more than one trigger, and a trigger may depend on a combination of conditions.

Stage 3 · Rule Interaction

Not a comparison of rules in the abstract. Rules are read together through the facts of a firm or transaction, where a single fact may connect to more than one rule. Each rule's Stage 1 and 2 reading, administering authority, and filing requirements are treated as inputs to this stage. The analysis identifies where rules connect to the firm and what relationship arises where they meet. Each row represents a connecting fact; the connections depend on the firm's circumstances.

Connecting facts

Fact about the firmQuestion
The firmWho it is: nationality, place of incorporation, place of establishment, ownership, or control.
The itemWhat it is and where it originates or sits: origin, content, technical characteristics, classification, or location.
The actWhat the firm does and where: export, re-export, transfer, provision, handling, service, and the place it happens.
The counterpartyWith whom: identity, ownership, designation, destination, or position in the transaction chain.
The useFor what: end use, end user, service purpose, or downstream activity.

Read each fact against every rule, one column per rule, noting whether and how the fact connects to that rule. The table extends to any number of rules. A row where two or more rules connect is a crossing point.

Relation at a crossing point

RelationReading
Overlap Multiple rules apply, and their requirements can be satisfied concurrently in the circumstances examined.
Operational Conflict Multiple rules apply, but their information, documentation, timing, or procedural requirements create additional coordination or decision demands.
Legal conflict Requirements cannot be satisfied concurrently in the relevant circumstances; satisfying one requires or produces non-compliance with another.

Which Rules Apply

Read who issues and administers each rule. Rules within the same government may share an institutional or regulatory context, including alignment between agencies. Rules from different governments arise from separate authorities and may lack a common institutional mechanism for resolving conflicting requirements. The relationship between the rules remains dependent on the specific crossing point and circumstances.

Stage 4 · Exposure Matrix

A matrix of variables describing a firm's exposure, decision requirements, and available responses before an outcome is known. The variables can be examined at different points in the rule's timeline and are informed by Stage 3 where more than one rule connects to the firm. The matrix does not presume a fixed outcome or cost level.

VariableReading
Position Relative to the Target Whether the firm is a direct target, a first-tier supplier or counterparty, or a third party connected through an indirect relationship.
Revenue share exposed Share of the firm's revenue associated with the affected item, transaction, counterparty, or market.
Available Substitutes Whether the firm can identify an alternative customer, supplier, counterparty, route, or market, and how long securing or switching to it would take.
Time Needed to Determine Applicability How long the firm needs to determine whether an item, counterparty, transaction, or activity falls within the rule, and whose information that determination depends on: the firm's own information (such as item specifications) or a counterparty's information (such as its ownership chain).
Time Before the Rule Applies How much time remains before the rule takes effect or a relevant deadline occurs, read against the applicable trigger and set against determination time and substitution lead time.
Advance preparation What preparatory actions are available or already underway before the relevant outcome: stockpiling, contract renegotiation, alternative arrangements, or license applications filed and pending.
Who Determines Applicability Whether one role assembles separate departmental readings and information into a consolidated determination, rather than whether each individual reading is correct.
