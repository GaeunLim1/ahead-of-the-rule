Stage 1 · Object of the Rule

What the text names as its target. Read from the article itself, before anything downstream is considered.

TypeTriggerExample
Attribute-based A continuous value measured on the item itself: content share, technical lineage, an aggregated count. A minimum content threshold by weight or value; a bulk-data threshold measured in persons (e.g. 100+ individuals' genomic records).
Relational A position in a network: nationality, headquarters location, a contract chain, a control relationship. Headquarters in a restricted jurisdiction; a re-export clause running through a third-country buyer; corporate affiliation extending to subsidiaries, parents, and successor entities.
Conduct-based A future behavior the text conditions on, not a present property. A disciplinary commitment made as a condition of a suspended denial order; a domestic-handling certification submitted as a condition of marketing approval.

Stage 2 · Design Layer

Not whether the rule existed before the case. Whether this rulemaking sits inside the agency's authorizing text, or redraws that line, and separately, what fires it.

Axis 1: scope of authority

TypeReading
Within the existing lineThe rulemaking moves inside the range already handed to the agency. Lower legal exposure once in force.
Redrawing the lineThe rulemaking extends the agency's own reach: a new jurisdictional or definitional line, not a new number inside an old one.

How the axis 1 reading is made

StepReading
Authorizing textThe provision that hands the agency its job, and how far that job runs. Cited directly, not summarized.
Stated aimWhat the agency says it is doing: preamble, stated rationale, legislative history.
Reach of the operative textWho the rule's own wording actually covers, read on its own, independent of what it says it is doing.
GapThe mismatch between the first two and the third. No mismatch: within the existing line. A mismatch that pulls in a new class of actor: redrawing the line.

Axis 2: trigger mechanism

TypeReading
Numeric triggerFires on a threshold or a calendar date, independent of any actor's behavior.
Conduct triggerFires on a breach or a failure to perform a condition. Creates a latent interval between the breach and its detection that a numeric trigger cannot produce.

The two axes, crossed

Within the existing lineRedrawing the line
Numeric triggerThreshold adjusted inside existing scope.New threshold extends jurisdiction itself.
Conduct triggerCompliance condition added inside existing scope.New conduct standard extends jurisdiction itself.

Stage 3 · Cross-Reading

Stage 1 and 2 read a single rule. This stage reads more than one together, not side by side.

Trigger

TypeReading
IndependentTwo or more rules each reach the firm on their own path.
EmbeddedOne rule's own clause names a second regime by reference: an exemption conditioned on another regulator's approval, say. Stage 1 still only picked one rule.

Object comparison

Do the rules target the same kind of fact, or different kinds? The comparison holds even when a single rule's own text names more than one kind at once. The object type predicts which part of the firm can see it first.

Object typeDefault owner
Attribute-basedThe function that measures the value (technical, data)
RelationalLegal and ownership diligence
Conduct-basedWhichever function owns that condition (regulatory affairs, information security)

Different object types default to different departments.

Design comparison

Did each rule redraw its line in the same direction, or differently. Two rules can move from territorial reach to a nexus test independently of each other, for unrelated stated reasons, and still end up catching the same third-country firm through separate paths. The comparison is not whether the rules coordinate; usually they do not.

Jurisdiction and licensing authority

QuestionReading
RegulatorWhich agency administers each rule.
Basis for reachWhat fact gives that regulator jurisdiction over this firm.
Filed whereWhere a license or disclosure under each rule is actually submitted.
CoordinationWhether the two regulators coordinate with each other. Usually they do not.
Cross-read riskWhere a filing made to satisfy one regulator can read badly to the other.

Axis cross-reading

A separate axis from object, design, and jurisdiction: the regulatory mission each rule serves. A gatekeeping-type rule (export control) and a validation-type rule (approval, certification) can reach the same firm with no shared vocabulary and no institutional reason to coordinate, whether they sit in the same country or different ones.

PairReading
Approval × RegulationWhether the conduct the export-control axis rewards (avoiding a named counterparty) is compatible with the condition the approval axis requires (e.g. data breadth, site access).
Approval × Deemed exportWhether preparing the approval filing itself (facility staffing, technology access) triggers a deemed-export disclosure independent of the filing's outcome.
Regulation × Deemed exportWhether the deemed-export exposure runs independent of the firm's designation status under the relational (export-control) axis. Territory and headcount, not corporate lineage, decide it.

Stage 4 · Cost Layer

It carries no fixed types. A label here would already be a result, and results are not available before a case closes. This layer is a matrix of variables that can be filled at any point, including before the outcome is known. It is informed by Stage 3, when more than one rule applies.

VariableReading
Distance from objectDirect target, first-tier supplier or counterparty, or a third party with only an indirect interest.
Revenue share exposedShare of that firm's revenue running through the named object.
Substitution availableWhether the firm can route around the object: an alternate customer, supplier, or market.
Prior positioningWhether the firm is already acting on the object before any outcome: stockpiling, contract renegotiation, license applications filed and pending.
Coordination pointWhether one role assembles the departments' separate readings into a single determination, not whether each reading is correct.

Scenarios

Named by condition, not by type: what the matrix looks like under each branch a rule's own timeline can take. When more than one rule applies, the branches cross.
