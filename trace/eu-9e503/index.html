One transaction, traced: EU 9E503.l

A new EU entry for gas-turbine compressor technology, followed through one design loop with a Korean partner. Status as of 14 October 2026.

EU engine makerEngineering site in the EU, sending compressor design data

Korean partnerIndependent firm, co-developing a civil gas turbine

Third-country buyerLater sales of the finished engine or its parts

The partner is not owned by the EU maker. The engine is not specially designed for military use; if it were, military controls would apply first.

1The change

A new entry controls the technology required to develop a high-loading axial compressor. It covers development only, and one of its key features rests on an undefined word.

Rule
New entry 9E503.l in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2021/821, added by Delegated Regulation C(2026) 6323. It covers aero, aero-derivative, marine and industrial gas turbines.
Whose rule
The EU's own. It sits in the "5xx" series of entries the EU adds outside the multilateral lists.
Status
Adopted 14 September 2026. It applies only after scrutiny and publication in the Official Journal; mid-November is expected, not fixed.
What is caught
Technology required for development, not production or use. The compressor must have one feature from l.1 and meet all four parameters in l.2.
The l.1 features
A multiple-stream fan system; feedback control of blade tip clearance; feedback management to avoid flutter; cooled gas path or rotating parts; tandem or splittered rotating aerofoils; or casings with non-axisymmetric geometry at the blade tip.
The l.2 thresholds
Average alternate stage loading of at least 0.22; that loading times 41.6 plus polytropic efficiency of at least 102; stability margin of at least 20%; average stage pressure ratio above 1.2.
Open term
"Feedback" is not defined, and l.1.b.1 and l.1.b.2 are the only features without a technical note. Scheduled clearance control and model-based estimates sit on the line.
Licence
Korea is not an EU001 destination. EU007 covers group transfers only, to wholly owned companies under an EU or EU001 parent. An independent partner needs an individual licence.

2Where it lands

The entry lands on one leg only: design data moving from the EU to Korea. It does not follow the data onward, and it does not touch the hardware.

EU to Korea

Design data sent by file, server access or a visit is an export of technology. The EU sender needs the licence; the Korean partner takes on its conditions by contract.

Inside Korea

Not an EU export. Sharing with Korean subcontractors or research partners is limited only by the licence conditions the partner accepted.

Korea onward

Outside EU law, which has no re-export control. Engines and parts built from the data are not caught by 9E503 at all, since it lists no goods.

Who classifies
Only the EU sender holds the design-point data the l.2 parameters need. The Korean partner cannot check the finding on its own.
Timing
Data sent after entry into force needs a licence, even under a contract signed before it.
OEM or ODM
Joint design is caught. Build-to-print production uses production technology, which the entry does not list.

3Beside Korean law

Korean law does not move with the EU change. The two rules overlap without conflict, and the Korean side has little to add.

Reach
9E503 is not in Korea's Annex 2, so the data is not a strategic item in Korea. A situational permit applies only if there is knowledge or notice of a WMD or missile end use. Annex 2-2 item 57 covers related tip-clearance technology, for Russia and Belarus only.
Timing
The EU date moves; the Korean list holds still until Korea amends it. Korea added the 2025 "5xx" entries, so 9E503 may follow.
Separate steps
Korea needs no permit to receive technology. An import purpose certificate, which could support the EU licence application, may not be available for an unlisted item.
Both at once
Yes. Nothing in Korean law forbids what the EU licence requires.

4What to do

  • Get the classification in writing. Ask the EU sender for its finding under 9E503.l and the l.2 basis for it.
  • Map the transfers. List the data the project will send after mid-November, and plan the licence lead time into the schedule.
  • Settle the licence route. An individual licence, unless the parties truly meet EU007's ownership and use conditions.
  • Record a reading of "feedback". Treat any closed-loop adjustment based on a measured or estimated state as caught, and note why.
  • Write the conditions into the contract. End use, onward sharing, rights in derived technology, and return or deletion at the end of the work.
