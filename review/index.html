US AFFILIATES RULE · ONE EXPORT ROUTE

The US Affiliates Rule on One Export Route: A Review

The US brings back the Affiliates Rule on who owns the recipient, from 10 November 2026. Here it is read on one export route from Korea, beside the rules that already attach to that route.

THE ROUTE IN THIS REVIEW Korea · exporter Country X · C Country Y · D

0 · Define the transaction

Rests on an assumption. Set for this review, not a fact from the rules. The ownership structure is Application example 1 in the text of the rule.

 This transaction
Item A part made for semiconductor manufacturing equipment, made in Korea. US-origin content is above the de minimis level, so the part is subject to the EAR.
Parties A Korean exporter. Company A is on the Entity List, needs a licence for all items subject to the EAR, and has a presumption of denial; it owns 35% of Company C. Company B is on the Entity List, needs a licence for items on the CCL, and is reviewed case by case; it owns 15% of Company C. Company C owns 50% of Company D. Neither C nor D is listed.
Route Leg 1: Korea to Country X, consigned to C as distributor. Leg 2: Country X to Country Y, to D as end user. FCA Korea: the buyer bears import duty in each country.
Timing The order is placed before 10 November 2026; the shipment leaves after it.

2 · See where they meet

Rests on the text of the rules and how they are built. Who reads each and where the data sits rest on the assumed chain.

 Korea strategic itemsUS Affiliates Rule
Type An export from Korea. A reexport from Korea, and again from X, of an item subject to the EAR.
What it reads The part’s technical specification; end use where the firm knows or is informed. Ownership share, traced and added up through C to D.
Parties Binds the Korean exporter. Ownership is not the test. Binds the Korean firm, and C when it reexports from X. C and D are caught through their owners.
Carry-over Applies at export from Korea. Follows the part to the reexport from X.
Checkpoint Before export At order and before each shipment, on each leg
Who in the firm reads it Engineering, classification Screening
Where the data sits Inside the firm Outside the firm
Who grants the licence The Korean licensing authority BIS
Relation Cumulative: a Korean licence does not clear BIS, and a BIS licence does not clear Korea. Same fact, two lists: the A, B, C, D chain is read by BIS against the Entity List and by OFAC against the SDN List; here only the first catches it. Incentive: the rule gives a reason to ask for a different consignee or a lower stake, and the red-flag review reads that request.
Tariff Attaches to both legs and does not move with this change.
Clears last The BIS licence on A’s terms. Its review policy is a presumption of denial, so the part may not leave at all.
