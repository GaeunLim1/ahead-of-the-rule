US AFFILIATES RULE · ONE EXPORT ROUTE

The US Affiliates Rule on One Export Route: A Review

The US Affiliates Rule reaches recipients through who owns them. In law it returns on 10 November 2026; an extension to 10 January 2027 has been announced but not published. Here it is followed through one export route from Korea under both dates.

THE ROUTE IN THIS REVIEW Korea · exporter Country X · C Country Y · D

1 · Analyze the change

Rests on the text of the rule, before and after the change, and the official record of when it applies.

The change

VariableUS Affiliates Rule
RuleExpanded: an existing set of listings reaches further. Interim final rule.
Whose ruleA country whose rule follows the item: the US. Added by the US alone, not agreed in a regime.
Status and datesAdopted and stayed. Fixed in law: the stay ends on 9 November 2026 and the rule returns on 10 November 2026. Announced: on 23 September 2026 the US and China agreed to extend the Busan arrangement to 10 January 2027, expected to carry the stay with it. No Federal Register notice of the extension found as of 12 October 2026.

2 · Place it on the transaction

Rests on an assumption. Set for this review, not a fact from the rules. The ownership structure is Application example 1 in the text of the rule.

The transaction

VariableThis transaction
ItemA part for semiconductor manufacturing equipment, made in Korea, with US content above the de minimis level. Assumed: a strategic item in Korea; on the CCL.
PartiesA Korean exporter. A, on the Entity List (license for all items subject to the EAR; presumption of denial), owns 35% of C. B, on the Entity List (license for CCL items; case by case), owns 15% of C. C owns 50% of D. C and D are not listed. What can move: a stake, or a listing.
Route and channelLeg 1: Korea to Country X, to C as distributor. Leg 2: Country X to Country Y, to D as end user, which happens only if the part leaves Korea. Goods only; no technology moves.
TimingOrder placed in October 2026; shipment planned for December 2026. Read under 10 November 2026 (in law) and 10 January 2027 (announced).

Where it lands

LegPointWhere the line lands
Leg 1: Korea to XExport under Korea’s rule; reexport under the EAR. Read by the Korean exporterLands here. C is inside: A and B together own exactly 50%, so a small sale by either moves C out. The exporter knows D is the end user, so D is read here too.
Leg 2: X to YReexport under the EAR, read by C. Korea’s rule does not reach this legLands here. D is inside: C owns exactly 50%, so a small sale by C moves D out.

4 · Turn it into action

Rests on the sections above, and on the compliance guidance in the sources.

VariableIn this transaction
Checks to redoTrace A, B, C and D tier by tier, and read the stakes again before the December shipment. If the stakes cannot be determined, Red Flag 29 applies.
Date exposurePlan on 10 November: the December shipment is held now; if the extension is published, two months of sales were held for nothing. Plan on 10 January: if no notice is published, the December shipment leaves without a required license. The fixed date is the safer plan; the wrong guess costs sales on one side and a violation on the other.
License pathA Korean license, and a BIS license on A’s terms with a presumption of denial. Ownership must be established first, then licensing; open-ended. The BIS license clears last, and may not clear at all.
ResponseShip before 10 November, while the stay holds: lawful, but it moves the timing of the transaction and should be recorded. Ship to D through another consignee: D is still a party, and it starts to look like circumvention.
Contract and recordsThe order was taken in October 2026, before either date. Assumed: no clause lets the exporter suspend for a new license requirement, so the risk of delay sits with the exporter. The ownership trace and the date the firm planned on are recorded.
