Three rules, one window

A new EU technology entry, the US Affiliates Rule and China's rare-earth controls, read on the same questions from the position of a Korean firm. Status as of 14 October 2026.

0Setting the comparison

The three rules hit different transactions. They are compared because they share a window, so the differences between them come from their design, not their timing.

The set
EU entry 9E503.l on gas-turbine compressor technology; the US Affiliates Rule extending end-user controls to 50%-owned affiliates; China's MOFCOM Announcement 61/2025 on rare-earth items made abroad.
The window
September 2026 to January 2027. Each rule reaches a decision point inside it.
As of
14 October 2026. Section 2 is the part to update when a status changes.
Vantage
A firm established in Korea, working as a partner, OEM or ODM, under Korea's Foreign Trade Act and its strategic items notice.

1Design: what each rule catches

Each rule ties itself to a transaction by a different hook: technology, ownership, or origin content. The further down that list, the further the rule follows goods abroad.

2Timing and how sure it is

The EU is uncertain only about when. The US and China are uncertain about when and whether, and they move together. Their defaults run in opposite directions: the EU rule does not apply until something happens; the other two return unless something happens.

3Reading the rule

The EU text leaves a key term undefined; the Chinese text has never been applied, so its methods are untested. The US rule is the easiest to read because it borrows a test already in use.

5What Korean law does

Nothing, for all three. Korea has no matching entry, no state assurance and no interpretive guidance to offer, so the Korean firm meets each foreign rule on that rule's own terms.

6Across the three

The US and Chinese rules mirror each other in form and timing. All three meet at the firm rather than at any one product.

Mirrors
Both the US and Chinese rules reach unlisted companies through 50% ownership, and each aims at the other side's listed companies. China's version came ten days after the US one.
Linked timing
Both were paused under the US–China arrangement of 30 October 2025, and both depend on the same truce. The EU change follows its own procedure.
Where they meet
The three rules catch different transactions. They meet at the firm: the two 50% rules screen the same customer base, all three fall in the same November-to-January calendar, and one compliance team handles them.
Overlap, not conflict
For a Korean firm's own trades, all three can be met at once, at a cost. A true conflict appears only for a subsidiary in China, where anti-sanctions rules can penalise cutting off a listed customer.

7Implications

  • Plan to each default. Wait for publication on the EU rule; prepare for the US and Chinese rules to return on 10 November, whatever the reports say.
  • Expect three roles. Recipient, screener and applicant each need data held by another party. Write that data flow into contracts before it is needed.
  • Read open terms conservatively. Where a term is undefined or a method untested, choose a reading, record why, and get the counterparty's in writing.
  • Korean listing would help Korean firms. Adding 9E503 to Korea's list would give them an import certificate to offer, as well as close the gap on re-export.
  • What to watch. Publication of the EU update; a BIS notice and a MOFCOM announcement before 10 November; 27 November for Announcement 46/2024.
